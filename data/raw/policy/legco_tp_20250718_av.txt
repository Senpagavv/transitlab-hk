       Legislative Council Panel on Transport
    Implementation of Smart Mobility Initiatives

PURPOSE

               This paper provides Members with an update on the latest
developments in various smart mobility initiatives, focusing on "Smart
Transport Infrastructure", "Data Sharing and Analytics", and "Technology
Application and Services".

BACKGROUND

2.  In December 2017, the HKSAR Government released the first

"Smart City Blueprint for Hong Kong", introducing a range of measures

across six smart domains - "Smart Mobility", "Smart Living", "Smart

Environment", "Smart People", "Smart Government", and "Smart

Economy" - to address urban management challenges and enhance citizens'

lives through innovative technology. Subsequently, the Government

published the "Smart Mobility Roadmap for Hong Kong" in July 2019,

followed by the "Smart City Blueprint for Hong Kong 2.0" in December

2020. In these updates, the Transport Department (TD) expanded the

Smart Mobility section by introducing new measures, such as establishing

the Smart Traffic Fund (the Fund) and promoting the trial and application

of autonomous vehicles (AV). These initiatives aim to optimize traffic

management, improve efficiency, and advance research and innovation in

vehicle-related technology.

UPDATE ON SMART MOBILITY INITIATIVES

3.  The Government has been actively advancing various smart

mobility initiatives by leveraging technologies such as innovative sensing

and detection, artificial intelligence, and big data analytics. These efforts

aim to address the growing demands of transportation and traffic, optimize

road use efficiency, and provide citizens with a more convenient and

seamless travel experience. Through the integration of "Smart Transport

Infrastructure", "Data Sharing and Analytics", and "Technology

(I) Smart Transport Infrastructure

4.       Smart transport infrastructure is a fundamental pillar of smart

mobility. It enhances road facilities through innovative technologies and

strengthens technical frameworks to collect, analyse, and process real-time

traffic data. This enables the dissemination of traffic information to road

users, optimising travel planning. The Government has been actively

advancing Hong Kong's smart transport infrastructure through various

initiatives, including promoting AV trials and applications, implementing

real-time adaptive traffic signal systems, advancing smart motorways, and

upgrading traffic control and surveillance systems.

Trial and Application of Autonomous Vehicles

5.       One of the key smart mobility measures outlined in the "Smart

Mobility Roadmap" and "Smart City Blueprint 2.0" is to keep pace with

the technological and industry advancements in vehicle-to-everything

(V2X) and AV. The Government completed the legislative amendment

process for the "Road Traffic (Amendment) (Autonomous Vehicles)

Ordinance 2023" and the "Road Traffic (Autonomous Vehicles) Regulation"

(Cap. 374AA) (AV Regulation) in May 2023 and January 2024,

respectively. The new AV regulatory framework was subsequently

implemented in March 2024, alongside the TD's release of the "Code of

Practice for Trial and Pilot Use of Autonomous Vehicles". Under this

framework, TD will issue "Pilot Licence" and "AV Certificate" to "Pilot

Proprietor" participating in the AV "Pilot Scheme". Based on the local

traffic conditions and input from key stakeholders, pilot proprietors will be

permitted to trial or operate one or more approved AVs in designated areas.

This approach provides the industry with greater flexibility to test and

deploy AV under more complex road conditions while maintaining road

safety.

6.       Several AV projects are currently in progress. As of the end of

June 2025, the TD has issued "Movement Permits" for 21 AV to undergo

trials at 12 different locations. At present, six AV are being trialed at four

         2
locations, including Tai Po Science Park, Fairview Park, the Hong Kong
Productivity Council, and Tuen Mun Lung Mun Road EcoPark. These
projects have demonstrated that trained autonomous driving systems can
adapt to Hong Kong's traffic patterns, with relevant technologies proving
extendable for AV trials on public roads. TD will actively engage with
project stakeholders regarding post-trial implementation. Additionally,
under the new AV Regulation enacted in 2024, TD has issued two AV Pilot
Licences - approving 30 AV for trial in North Lantau and two autonomous
light buses for trial in the West Kowloon Cultural District (WKCD).

7.  The North Lantau project, launched in December 2024, has

made significant progress. Under the new regulatory framework, the TD

has streamlined the approval process while maintaining rigorous oversight,

actively facilitating licencees and granting approvals. Within months,

several technical advancements have been achieved, including scaling the

trial from a single vehicle to 10 vehicles operating simultaneously to

collect multi-vehicle data, conducting passenger-carrying trials on specific

routes following safety assessments, expanding the trial scope to more

complex roads, and increasing the speed limit to 50 km/h based on the trial

route's maximum speed limit. Meanwhile, the WKCD project will trial

autonomous light buses equipped with an autonomous driving system

designed to comply with Hong Kong's left-hand traffic rule. The

research institute plans to conduct passenger-carrying trials on public roads

within WKCD by the end of this year.

8.  Looking ahead, the Government remains committed to

prioritizing safety while leveraging project insights to refine technical

standards and steadily advance AV development. In addition to the

aforementioned initiatives, we are actively progressing other projects,

including autonomous light buses operating between Hong Kong Science

Park in Sha Tin and MTR University Station, as well as an autonomous

transportation system connecting SKYCITY at Hong Kong International

Airport with the Hong Kong Port Island of the Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macao

Bridge. The goal is to implement autonomous driving services in local

communities within the next one to two years.

Real-time Adaptive Traffic Signal System

9.  As real-time adaptive traffic signal technology continues to

mature, its applications have expanded globally. Many cities in the

Mainland are actively integrating this technology, using artificial

intelligence to analyze traffic conditions and adjust signals in real time to

optimize timing for signalised junctions, effectively reducing road

    3
congestion. The TD successfully completed the pilot scheme for the
"Real-time Adaptive Traffic Signal System" at five independent signalised
junctions as early as 2021. Additionally, in 2022, TD began installing and
trialing the system at eight linked signalised junctions in Tung Chung town
center. The trial was completed in 2024 and is currently operating
smoothly. Data indicates that following the installation of the system at
the tested junctions, the average waiting time for vehicles passing through
was reduced by approximately 5-10%, significantly improving the
operational efficiency of the junctions.

10.  Building on previous successes, the TD is expanding the system

to approximately 50 suitable independent signalised junctions across Hong

Kong. The project secured funding approval from the Finance

Committee in June 2024, and the related consultancy study commenced in

July 2024. System installation is expected to begin in 2026, followed by

testing and gradual deployment. The entire project is anticipated to be

completed by 2027.

11.  Meanwhile, the TD is conducting further trials of the system at

busier linked signalised junctions (such as Wan Chai North and Causeway

Bay). At the same time, it is exploring the use of other relevant

technologies (including those from the Mainland), to support the broader

application of the system at suitable junctions. As part of the

Government's development of new urban areas, TD will collaborate with

the relevant departments overseeing infrastructure projects to integrate the

system into signalised junctions from the outset. For instance, the first

batch of systems in the Tung Chung New Town Extension is expected to

be installed by 2026.

Traffic Control and Surveillance Systems

12.  To ensure the safe and efficient operation of Hong Kong's

strategic roads, all government tunnels and major trunk roads are equipped

with Traffic Control and Surveillance Systems (TCSS), which operate

continuously. These systems include lane control signals, variable

message signs, closed-circuit televisions (CCTVs), and automatic incident

detection systems to support real-time traffic management and monitoring.

Government departments rely on TCSS for 24-hour surveillance of road

traffic in tunnels and major trunk roads, enabling swift response to

incidents by notifying tunnel staff and the Police. Meanwhile, lane

control signals and variable message signs help direct motorists to reduce

speed or take alternative routes, ensuring road user safety and minimizing

the broader impact of traffic disruptions.

pilot projects (see paragraph 15 below) aimed at accelerating the

innovative development of smart motorway management.

Smart Motorway Pilot Scheme at Ting Kau Bridge

15.  "Moving Towards the Application of Smart Motorway

Management" is one of the key initial recommendations of the "Traffic and

Transport Strategy Study". This initiative aims to leverage technology to

maximize the use of limited road resources and strengthen the resilience of

transport infrastructure in responding to incidents. To support this effort,

the TD launched a Pilot Scheme on 29 December 2024, at Ting Kau Bridge

southbound to test smart motorway technology and refine detailed

operational arrangements.

16.  From the commencement of the Pilot Scheme until the end of

June this year, a total of 19 minor traffic incidents occurred in the

designated road section. Of these, 13 were successfully detected within

10 seconds 2 by the automatic incident detection system, which

simultaneously issued alerts to the operational staff of the Tsing Ma

Control Area. This enhancement has significantly improved the

efficiency of traffic incident management and eased traffic flow.

(II) Data Sharing and Analytics

17.  As smart transport infrastructure projects advance, the resulting

traffic data has become essential for enhancing transport system planning,

management efficiency, and operational resilience. The Government

actively leverages data analytics to gain insights into citizens' travel habits

and transport mode trends, supporting more effective transport planning

and traffic management. Additionally, traffic data is made accessible

through various platforms, including the TD's "HKeMobility" mobile

application and the "Traffic Data Analytics System", which provide real-

time and forecast traffic data alongside public transport information.

These initiatives foster the development of more innovative and

personalized services, facilitate citizens' journey planning and public

transport use, and promote cross-sector applications in smart mobility.

"HKeMobility" Mobile Application

18.  Since its 2021 update, the TD has enhanced the "HKeMobility"

mobile application to provide personalized traffic and public transport

information, including expanded real-time arrival data. These features

help citizens estimate journey times, make informed travel decisions, and

choose optimal departure times. TD has continuously improved the app's

functionality by integrating real-time toll information for Tai Lam Tunnel

and road harbor crossings, queue ticket distribution and calling status for

TD's licensing offices, and an enhanced user interface. Most of the data

available on "HKeMobility" is also shared via the Government's "Open

Data Portal" (data.gov.hk) and the "Common Spatial Data Infrastructure

Portal" (portal.csdi.gov.hk). Since its launch, the mobile application has

gained widespread adoption, with over 2.84 million downloads and an

average daily hit rate of 190 000 as of June 2025. Moving forward, we

Installation of Information Display Panels at Sheltered Bus Stops and
Covered Public Transport Interchanges

19.  To enhance passenger convenience and help them better manage

waiting times and travel plans, the Government has been subsidising

franchised bus companies on a matching basis since 2016 to accelerate the

installation of real-time bus arrival information display panels at sheltered

bus stops where technically feasible (including locations with power

supply and sufficient space). As of June 2025, franchised bus companies

have installed information display panels at 1 114 sheltered bus stops and

Government-owned covered public transport interchanges. The TD and

franchised bus companies will continue to assess the feasibility of

expanding the installation of real-time bus arrival information display

panels at additional sheltered bus stops as appropriate.

(III) Technology Application and Services

20.  By integrating smart transport infrastructure with relevant data,

the Government leverages technology to enhance traffic management

measures and policies. These initiatives aim to improve transport

efficiency, promote road safety, and provide greater convenience to citizens.

Key projects include the adoption of automated parking systems, the

expansion of advanced vehicle technology applications, the

implementation of traffic e-enforcement system, and the introduction of

electronic licensing initiatives to digitize transport services and enhance

the overall travel experience.

utilization and increases parking density, helping to address public demand

for parking spaces. The Government is actively promoting these systems

in suitable public and short-term tenancy car parks while encouraging their

adoption in private car parks. Regarding short-term tenancy car parks,

the Government has launched three automated parking system projects3,

providing approximately 180 automated parking spaces. The automated

Advanced Vehicle Technologies

23.        The TD regularly reviews and updates regulations and guidelines,

referencing international standards to align with the latest automotive

market developments. Earlier this year, the Government introduced

legislative amendments to optimize vehicle design and construction

standards, facilitating the adoption of new automotive technologies in

Hong Kong to enhance road safety and convenience. Since March 2025,

drivers have been permitted to use remote control parking functions and

access non-driving-related information on in-vehicle visual display units.

24.        Existing regulations and approvals from the TD allow vehicle

manufacturers to equip vehicles with Level 2 advanced driver assistance

systems (ADAS), which are widely used in the market. These systems

include remote control parking, non-driving-related information in visual

display units, automatic parking, vehicle side collision warning, automatic

anti-skid, assisted direction guidance, automatic lane changing, lane

Traffic e-Enforcement System

25.  To enhance the efficiency of traffic enforcement through

technology and provide more convenient public services, the Government

completed legislative amendments for electronic traffic enforcement in

2024. These amendments enable the Hong Kong Police Force to issue

fixed penalty notices (e-tickets) for traffic violations electronically. Since

November 18, 2024, the TD has been collecting verified electronic contact

details from registered vehicle owners, driving licence holders, and permit

holders to facilitate the issuance of e-tickets. Beginning 15 June 2025,

the Police will progressively implement the e-ticket system and

simultaneously launch the "Electronic Traffic Ticket Platform" website

(www.etrafficticket.gov.hk) and mobile application. These platforms

will allow citizens to access their e-tickets and conveniently pay fines using

newly introduced electronic payment methods.

Electronic Licensing Initiatives

26.  To support the Government's "Be the Smart Regulator" initiative

and address the growing demand for licensing services, the TD has

introduced electronic licensing measures and simplified application

processes. Currently, TD offers over 20 online services (including high-

demand services such as vehicle licence renewals, full driving licence

renewals, and international driving permit (IDP) applications) allowing

citizens to submit applications via mobile devices or computers using

"iAM Smart" digital signatures and/or e-Form functions, eliminating the

need to visit licensing offices and wait for counter services. TD plans to

further expand these services by adding an additional 20 licensing options,

including applications for excess dimension permits and Certificates of

Driving Licence Particulars.

27.  The TD has been progressively rolling out various electronic

licensing initiatives to improve operational efficiency, enhance public

convenience, and reduce congestion and wait times at licensing offices.

These initiatives include the enhanced Vehicle Licence (eVL), the "E-

Licensing Portal" one-stop platform 5 , IDP Self-Service Kiosks 6 , "e-
Auction" 7 and the electronic driving licence (eDL). Launched in
December 2024, the eVL benefits over 800,000 vehicle owners by
eliminating the need to replace the paper vehicle licence upon renewal after
initially obtaining a new licence without a printed expiry date.
Additionally, TD has simplified the required application documents and
implemented artificial intelligence technology to assist in processing
online vehicle licence renewals, aiming to reduce processing times of
applications that do not require manual verification of supporting
documents from 10 working days to just 3. As application and processing
procedures become increasingly automated, more citizens are expected to
apply for vehicle licences online, avoiding the need for in-person visits to
TD licensing offices. Furthermore, following the passage of relevant
amendment regulations by the Legislative Council on 12 June, TD will
introduce eDL in September this year, allowing licence holders to choose
to display their eDL on smartphones. This measure is expected to provide
convenience to approximately 2.5 million licence holders. Meanwhile,
the Government plans to submit legislative amendments regarding the
International Circulation Permit to the Legislative Council within this year
to streamline application and processing procedures, facilitating electronic
applications for the applicants.

WAY FORWARD

29.  Looking ahead, the Government will continue to actively

promote smart mobility by consolidating existing strategies, drawing on

global experiences, adopting appropriate new technologies, and launching

projects that enhance convenience for road users. The TD initiated the

Traffic and Transport Strategy Study at the end of 2021 to develop a long-

term Transport Strategy Blueprint for Hong Kong. Nine initial

recommendations were introduced in December 2023, and TD has been

progressively implementing smart mobility pilot schemes since 2024. In

collaboration with relevant policy bureaux and departments, we aim to

deepen various studies and finalize the Transport Strategy Blueprint this

year to maintain a reliable, safe, smart, environmentally friendly, and

highly efficient transport system for Hong Kong.
