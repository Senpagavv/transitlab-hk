   INTRODUCTION

   At the meeting of the Executive Council on 14 May 2024, the

   Council ADVISED and the Chief Executive ORDERED that the Road Traffic

   (Public Service Vehicles) (Amendment) Regulation 2024 (the Amendment

A  Regulation), at Annex A, should be made under section 7(1C) of the Road

   Traffic Ordinance (Cap. 374) (the Ordinance) to give effect to the revised rate

   of fares for the hiring of urban, NT and Lantau taxis with effect from 14 July

   2024, as follows ­

   (a) an increase of $2 in flagfall charge;

   (b) an increase of $0.2 per jump in the incremental charge for the first-
         tier distance1 after flagfall; and

   (c) an increase of $0.1 per jump in the incremental charge for the
         second-tier distance2 after flagfall.

   1 Under the Government's proposed scale of fares, the incremental charge for the first-tier distance applies
       to each jump per 200 metres (m) or every one minute not in motion travelled for ­
       (a) 2 to 9 kilometres (km) for urban taxis (or until the chargeable amount reaches $102.5);
       (b) 2 to 8 km for NT taxis (or until the chargeable amount reaches $82.5); and
       (c) 2 to 20 km for Lantau taxis (or until the chargeable amount reaches $195).

   2 Under the Government's proposed scale of fares, the incremental charge for the second-tier distance
       applies to each jump per 200 m or every one minute not in motion travelled for ­
       (a) after 9 km for urban taxis (or after the chargeable amount reaches $102.5);
       (b) after 8 km for NT taxis (or after the chargeable amount reaches $82.5); and
       (c) after 20 km for Lantau taxis (or after the chargeable amount reaches $195).

JUSTIFICATIONS

(A) Government's Consideration

2.      Taxis provide personalised point-to-point public transport service.

In recent years, there have been calls from both the community and the taxi

trade for the Government to implement initiatives to enhance the quality and

sustainability of taxi services. In view of the expectation of the general

public and the requests from the taxi trade, the Government had earlier on

reviewed the overall taxi operation and management, and launched a series of

measures with a view to enhancing the overall quality of personalised point-

to-point transport services and promoting the healthy development of the taxi

industry in the long run. Such measures include introducing a taxi fleet

regime, increasing the maximum passenger seating capacity of taxis from five

to six, and introducing a Taxi-Driver-Offence Points (TDOP) system and a

two-tier penalty system for certain taxi-driver-related offences. The relevant

bills3 were passed by the Legislative Council (LegCo) in mid-December 2023

and gazetted on 22 December 2023. It is expected that the implementation

of the above measures (the latest progress of which is elaborated in paragraphs

18 to 19 below) will help enhance the service quality and overall image of the

taxi trade, thereby increasing the patronage and attracting more newcomers to

join the industry. Apart from these measures, it is also important to review

the taxi fares and the financial viability of taxi operation at appropriate

junctures in order to ensure the healthy development of the taxi trade in the

long run and maintain a stable supply of taxi services to cater for passengers'

needs.

3.      The established policy is to consider taxi fare increase applications

in accordance with the following five key criteria ­

    (a) the need to ensure the financial viability of taxi operation, taking into
          consideration changes in revenue and operating costs;

    (b) the need to maintain an acceptable level of taxi service in terms of
          vehicle availability, passenger waiting time and passengers'
          feedback on service;

    (c) the need to maintain a reasonable differential between taxi fares and

(C) Operation Status of the Taxi Trade

7.  At present, there are a total of 18 163 taxis, among which 15 250 are

urban taxis, 2 838 are NT taxis and 75 are Lantau taxis. As at the end of

January 2024, about 59% of taxis are owned by individuals whilst the

remaining are owned by companies. The taxi trade mainly consists of rentee-

drivers, rentor-owners and owner-drivers 9 . The TD conducts regular

taximeter reading surveys, obtains the price of Liquefied Petroleum Gas

(LPG) from the LPG suppliers, and collects information on taxi drivers'

operation through questionnaire surveys. Furthermore, the TD gathers taxi

operating data from taxi owners and trade associations through questionnaire

surveys10.

8.  After the last taxi fare increase which took effect in July 202211, taxi

operation had still been affected by the epidemic for certain months and the

overall public transport patronage only gradually recovered since the re-

opening of the border with the Mainland in February 2023. In view of this,

instead of comparing the operation in 2023 with that of 2022, during which

the taxi operating data could be distorted by the unstable operating

environment, comparisons were made between the situation in 2019 (i.e. pre-

epidemic) and 2023 so as to reflect more accurately the actual operation status

of the taxi trade at present.

12. As shown from the above data, the taxi trade is facing genuine
difficulty in maintaining the financial viability of its operation at the current
taxi fare level. The taxi trade is of the view that an increase in taxi fares
would raise the income of front-line drivers and attract more newcomers to
join the industry, whilst at the same time encouraging the trade to improve
their services and invest more in new facilities, thereby enhancing the overall
taxi service quality and fostering the healthy development of the trade in the
long run.

13. Having regard to the above, we consider that there is a case to
increase the fares for urban, NT and Lantau taxis. Nevertheless, we are of
the view that the proposed increases by the taxi trade are on the high side and
would be difficult to be accepted by the public. According to the trade's
proposals, the average rates of increase for urban, NT and Lantau taxis would

than the forecast inflation rate of 4.7%14 (comparing the 12-month period

           NT taxis, and we see no reason why Lantau taxis should charge
           passengers differently.

OTHER ISSUES
Taxi Rental

17.  There are concerns about how taxi rental will change after the

adjustment of taxi fares. Taxi rental has all along been a commercial

arrangement between owners and drivers under a free market. The rental

level fluctuates with the market supply and demand, and is affected by other

factors such as the vehicle types, age and maintenance condition of individual

taxis. Generally, taxi owners will observe the changes in business and

drivers' income during the initial period of taxi fare adjustment, before

discussing rental adjustment with drivers' groups having regard to the actual

market situation. Take the last taxi fare increase as an example. Although

taxi owners gradually raised the taxi rental after the fare increase in July 2022,

the rental has not yet resumed to the 2019 level to date. This shows that, in

adjusting taxi rental, taxi owners will consider relevant factors including the

acceptability of drivers and the manpower supply in the trade.

Improving Driving Safety of Taxis

21.  As mentioned above, taxi insurance premiums increased sharply

between 2019 and 2023. The underwriting decisions and determination of

the premium level are made by insurance companies based on commercial

principles in entering into policy contracts with the policyholders and through

adjustments in the free market. Premium levels of taxi insurance reflect the

risk associated with underwriting taxi business, which are affected by factors

including taxi accident rates, claims history, etc. The underwriting loss

recorded in the taxi insurance business over the years reflects a mismatch

between underwriting risks and premium levels.

22.  The Sixth LegCo formed a Joint Subcommittee on Issues Relating

to Insurance Coverage for the Transport Sector (the Subcommittee) in January

2021 to explore the problems encountered by the taxi trade when procuring

motor insurance. The Subcommittee suggested the taxi trade to adopt

measures to strengthen the management of taxis and improve driving safety

of taxi drivers. To promote the work in this respect, the Government and the

Insurance Authority have been following up on the implementation of the

Subcommittee's recommendations and actively meeting with the

representatives of the taxi trade and insurance sector to discuss issues relating

to the application of technology to improve the risk management of taxis, etc.

The Government has also been supportive of the taxi trade in enhancing the

safety standard of their services and improving management quality, so as to

reduce the risks associated with underwriting taxi insurance at source. It is

understood that at present, there are insurance companies offering discounts

on insurance premiums and lower deductibles, etc., so as to encourage the

installation of technological devices in taxis in enhancing driving safety and

the reporting of drivers' information and so forth.

23.  Moreover, upon commencement of taxi fleet operation, the

professional fleet management of fleet taxis, together with the installation of

safety-related devices (including global positioning system, dash cameras and

safety devices that monitor drivers' driving performance) in fleet taxis by fleet

operators, should be conducive to reducing traffic accidents at source. The

Government also believes that fleet taxis would set a leading and exemplary

role in the trade, thereby encouraging more taxis to apply technology to

improve driving safety and creating room for reduction in premiums and

improvement in the operation of taxis.

PUBLIC CONSULTATION

27.  We consulted the Panel on Transport of LegCo on the taxi trade's

fare increase applications on 12 April 2024. Quite a number of the Members

noted the difficult business environment faced by the taxi trade and considered

it not unreasonable to increase taxi fares. Nevertheless, they considered the

rates of fare increase proposed by the taxi trade were too high to be acceptable

to the public, and expected the Government to perform its gatekeeping role to

contain the rates of increase at a reasonable level. Some Members

considered that the taxi trade should display greater efforts to improve the

quality of taxi service and driving safety in order to justify the increase in taxi

fares. A few members were also concerned that the taxi owners, instead of

the frontline drivers, would benefit from the fare increase as the owners would

increase the rental after the taxi fare increase.

    Sustainability, Economic and Family Implications

Sustainability Implications

          The proposal helps relieve the mounting pressure brought by
increasing operating costs being faced by taxi operators and improve the
financial viability of taxi operation. Nevertheless, the proposed fare
increases would bring extra cost to taxi passengers, in particular to those
middle-income groups who use taxi services more frequently.

Economic Implications

2.  With taxi fares' weighting in CCPI at 0.34%, the proposed

increases in flagfall charge and subsequent incremental charge for the

urban, NT and Lantau taxis with effect from 14 July 2024 are expected to

have a negligible impact on the underlying CCPI inflation, estimated at

0.01 percentage point in 2024, and 0.02 percentage point in 2025. The

impact would vary slightly among the three underlying Consumer Price

Index (CPI) sub-indices1, ranging from 0.01 percentage point for CPI(A)

to 0.02 percentage point for CPI(C) in both 2024 and 2025.

Family Implications

3.  Since the proposal may bring additional income to some frontline

taxi drivers, it may have positive implications to the financial abilities of

their families. While it may bring extra cost to taxi passengers, in

particular those middle-income families who may use taxi services more

frequently, the impact should be mild.

                        Taxi Fare Increase Applications

         The urban, New Territories ("NT") and Lantau taxi trade reached
a consensus on their respective fare increase applications in March 2023,
requesting for an increase in the flagfall charges, incremental charges
after flagfall, as well as several additional fares; the urban taxi also asked
for shortening the waiting time1. According to the proposal put forward
by the trades, the average fare increase rates are 16.95% for urban taxis,
15.37% for NT taxis, and 11.68% for Lantau taxis. The applications
were considered by the Transport Advisory Committee ("TAC") at its
meeting held on 16 April 2024. This letter sets out TAC's advice.

1 Different taxi associations had submitted various fare increase proposals to the Transport
    Department ("TD") between November 2022 and January 2023. They later informed the TD in
    March 2023 that the following were the proposals agreed by the urban, NT and Lantau taxi trades ­
    (a) increasing the flagfall charge by $5, $4.5 and $6 respectively;
    (b) increasing the incremental charges for the first-tier distance after flagfall by $0.2, $0.2 and $0.1
         respectively, and those for the second-tier distance after flagfall by $0.2, $0.2 and $0.2
         respectively;
    (c) increasing the additional fare for every animal or bird carried ($2 increase for urban and NT
         taxis; and $3 increase for Lantau taxis);
    (d) increasing the additional fare for every article of baggage carried ($1 increase for urban and NT
         taxis; and $2 increase for Lantau taxis);
    (e) For urban taxis, shortening the waiting time for a hired taxi which is not in motion from 60
         seconds to 45 seconds per jump; and
    (f) For Lantau taxis, increasing the additional fare for every hiring arranged through telephone
         booking by $3.

Basis of TAC's consideration

2. When considering the applications, TAC acknowledged that taxis
provided a personalised point-to-point public transport service, and that
the established policy was to consider taxi fare increase applications in
accordance with the following major criteria ­

   (a) the need to ensure the financial viability of taxi operation, taking
          into consideration changes in revenue and operating costs;

   (b) the need to maintain an acceptable level of taxi service in terms
          of vehicle availability, passenger waiting time and passengers'
          feedback on service;

   (c) the need to maintain a reasonable differential between taxi fares
          and those of other public transport modes2;

   (d) public acceptability of the proposed fares; and

   (e) taxi fare structure which should be "front-loaded" and thereafter
          on a varying descending scale for incremental charges.

Latest operation status of the taxi trade

3. TAC Members noted that, after netting off inflation, the daily
meter revenue of rentee-drivers of urban and NT taxis in 2023 increased
by about 0.5% to 1.1% as compared with that in 2019 (i.e. pre-epidemic),
while that of rentee-drivers of Lantau taxis dropped by about 5%. As
for cost expenses, the fuel cost in 2023 increased by about 19% to 35% as
compared with that of 2019, whereas taxi rental decreased by about 2% to
12%. In sum, as compared with 2019, after netting off inflation, the
average monthly net income of rentee-drivers of urban and NT taxis
slightly increased by about 6.2% and 0.8% respectively, while that of
rentee-drivers of Lantau taxis dropped by about 2.7%. In terms of
absolute value, the average monthly net income of rentee-drivers in 2023
was around $19,100 to $21,800, which was lower than the average
monthly salary of the transport trade in the fourth quarter of 2023
(i.e. $24,800) as announced by the Census and Statistics Department.

2 TAC's advice on the fare differential between taxis and other modes of public transport is five to
    seven times for urban taxis and three to four times for NT taxis.

4. As for taxi owners, TAC Members noted that the operating
expenses in 2023 have risen significantly from the 2019 level, including a
surge of nearly 60% in insurance premiums and an average increase of
about 30% in repair and maintenance fees, which resulted in a significant
increase in the operating costs and financial burden of taxi owners. On
the other hand, to avoid idling of taxis, taxi owners could only rent out
their taxis at rates lower than those of 2019. The surging operating
costs, coupled with shrinking rental income, led to a plunge in the
average monthly net income (after netting off inflation) of rentor-owners
and owner-drivers in 2023 as compared with that in 2019. The average
monthly net income of rentor-owners and owners-drivers decreased by
about 33% to 48% and 10% to 13% respectively.

5. TAC Members noted from the data above that the taxi trade
might find it difficult to maintain the financial viability of their operation
at the current taxi fare level. TAC Members also noted that the trade
hoped that the increase in taxi fares would raise the income of front-line
drivers and attract more newcomers to join the industry, whilst at the
same time encouraging the trade to improve their services and invest
more in new facilities, thereby enhancing the overall taxi service quality
and fostering the healthy development of the taxi trade in the long run.

TAC's advice

6. Having balanced the various factors, TAC members
acknowledged that there were reasonable grounds to increase the fares for
urban, NT and Lantau taxis, but at a rate lower than the trades' proposals.
According to the proposals of the trades, the average rates of the fare
increase are 16.95% for urban taxis, 15.37% for NT taxis and 11.68% for
Lantau taxis, with a weighted average rate of increase of 16.68%. TAC
Members considered the rates proposed by the trade were too high and
would not be accepted by the public.

7. Having considered the latest operation status of the taxi trade and
public acceptability, as well as the relevant factors mentioned in
paragraph 2 above, TAC Members supported the Government's
recommendation of a $2 increase in the flagfall charge, a $0.2 increase
in the incremental charge for the first-tier distance after flagfall, and
a $0.1 increase in the incremental charge for the second-tier distance
after flagfall, for urban, NT and Lantau taxi fares alike. The average
rates of the recommended fare increase will be 8.86% for urban taxis,
9.56% for NT taxis and 10.92% for Lantau taxis, with a weighted average
                                                 -4-

rate of increase of 8.98%. TAC Members noted that under the
Government's recommendation, the fare differential between taxis and
other public transport modes would be within or close to the established
ranges.

8. As regards the urban taxi trade's proposal to shorten the waiting
time from 60 seconds to 45 seconds, TAC Members noted that there
would already be compensation for taxi drivers for waiting in congested
areas with the proposed higher incremental charges after flagfall, and as
such there was no need for, in effect, a double increase. As for the
trades' proposal to increase the additional fares for every article of
baggage carried and for every animal or bird carried, TAC Members
noted that there was no particular trend of change in carriage of baggage,
animal or bird by taxi passengers for justifying adjustments to the
relevant additional fares. Regarding the proposal of the Lantau taxi
trade to increase the additional fare for every hiring arranged through
telephone booking, TAC Members noted that the proposal would deviate
from existing arrangement for the urban and NT taxis and there was no
reason for Lantau taxis to be treated differently.

9. In the course of discussing the fare increase applications, TAC
Members also noted that the Government had launched a series of
measures to enhance the overall quality of personalised point-to-point
transport service. Such measures include introducing a taxi fleet
regime; increasing the maximum passenger seating capacity of taxis from
five to six; introducing a Taxi-Driver-Offence Points ("TDOP") system
and a two-tier penalty system for certain taxi-driver-related offences, etc.
The relevant bills were passed by the Legislative Council in
mid-December 2023 and gazetted on 22 December 2023. In addition,
the Government has commenced a study to explore whether it would be
feasible to require all taxis in Hong Kong to be installed with camera
system in the compartment, and plans to consult the taxi trade within this
year. The Government will also continue to encourage the trade to
enhance taxi driving safety through various measures. TAC noted that
with the implementation of the aforesaid measures, the overall quality of
taxi service should be enhanced.
